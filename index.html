The History of the United States

From the first Indigenous nations and colonial settlements to modern democracy, the United States has grown through struggle, innovation, leadership, and change.

Explore the Timeline Learn More

Overview

The United States began as a collection of colonies under British rule and became an independent nation in 1776. Over more than two centuries, it expanded across the continent, endured civil conflict, shaped global industry, and evolved toward a more inclusive democratic society.

Colonial Beginnings

European settlers arrived in North America beginning in the 1500s and 1600s, establishing colonies that grew into a diverse society shaped by trade, faith, labor, and conflict.

Independence

The Declaration of Independence in 1776 marked the beginning of a new nation, inspired by Enlightenment ideas about liberty, equality, and self-government.

National Growth

The country expanded westward, built railroads, developed industry, and faced major questions about slavery, citizenship, and the meaning of equality.

Major Timeline

1776

The Declaration of Independence was adopted, formally beginning the United States.

1787

The U.S. Constitution was created, establishing the framework for the federal government.

1803

The Louisiana Purchase doubled the nation's size and opened the way for westward expansion.

1861–1865

The Civil War challenged the country’s future and led to the end of slavery.

1914–1918

The United States played a major role in World War I and emerged as a global power.

1960s

The Civil Rights Movement pushed the nation toward greater equality and justice.

Key Eras in American History

Several important periods shaped the character, identity, and political structure of the United States.

Founding Era

The nation was created through revolution, constitutional debate, and the effort to build a government based on representative democracy.

Expansion Era

Territories were added, settlers moved west, and the country grew physically and economically.

Modern America

The 20th and 21st centuries brought industrial growth, social movements, technology, and continuing struggles for civil rights and equality.

“The United States is a nation of ideals, shaped by courage, conflict, and the ongoing effort to make liberty and justice real for all people.”

Why It Matters

The story of the United States is not just about events—it is about people, ideas, and values that continue to influence the world today.

Democratic Ideas

The Constitution and Bill of Rights helped shape ideas about rights, citizenship, and limited government.

Innovation

American industry, science, and technology helped transform transportation, communication, and daily life around the world.

Change and Inclusion

The struggle for equality has shaped American culture, politics, and law, making the country more reflective of its people.
